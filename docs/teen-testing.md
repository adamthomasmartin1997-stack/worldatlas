# Playtest script: Worldwise with teenagers

A first, informal test with 4–6 teenagers aged 13–16. The goal is to see where the site confuses or bores people and what makes them want to come back, not to grade them. Allow about 35 minutes per person.

## Before you start

- **Who**: 4–6 young people aged 13–16, a mix of those who like geography and history and those who don't.
- **What you need**: the preview link on the phone they normally use, this script, a timer, and a notes sheet per person (see the end).
- **Consent and safety**: get written agreement from a parent or carer for anyone under 16, and the young person's own agreement. Run sessions somewhere open, or with two adults present. Write no names on notes (use P1, P2…), and don't photograph or record faces. Anyone can stop at any time. This is general good practice, not legal advice; follow your school's or organisation's own rules if they apply.

## Say this at the start

"We're testing the website, not you. There are no wrong answers. If something is confusing or boring, that's really useful to know. Please think out loud: say what you're looking at and what you expect to happen. I mostly won't help, because we want to see what happens without me."

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

- What do you use when you need to look something up for homework?
- Any games or apps you play to learn things? What makes you keep playing?

## Tasks (25 minutes)

For each task, read the words in quotes, then watch. If they're stuck for more than a minute, give a small hint and note that you did.

1. **First impressions** (home page, 30 seconds of looking): "What do you think this site is for? What would you tap first?" Watch whether they see it's for learning and games, and what draws the eye.
2. **Find a fact**: "Find the capital of a country you'd like to visit, and one surprising fact about it." Watch search vs map vs browsing, and whether they find "Did you know?".
3. **Today's challenge**: "Play today's challenge." Watch whether the goal is clear without explanation.
4. **Mistakes**: "Play Flags on Hard for a few questions. Then find where your mistakes went." Watch whether they read the mistake list and find Review or the weak-spots reminder.
5. **History**: "Pick a time in history you've studied at school. Find out what else was happening in the world at that time." Watch whether they use eras or Meanwhile, and whether the year slider makes sense.
6. **Timeline builder**: "Play one round on Easy." Watch whether moving the cards is obvious on a phone.
7. **Data**: "Compare your country with one you think is very different. What surprised you?"

Useful prompts while they work: "What are you looking for?", "What do you think will happen if you tap that?", "Is that what you expected?"

## Afterwards (5 minutes)

Rate 1 (not at all) to 5 (very):
- It was fun.
- It was easy to use.
- I learned something new.
- I'd use it for homework.
- I'd come back to it on my own.

Then ask:
- What was the best bit?
- What was boring or confusing?
- If you could add one thing, what would it be?
- Which game would you play again?
- Would you tell a friend about it? Why or why not?
